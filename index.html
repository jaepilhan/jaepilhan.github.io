Dr. Jaepil Han

I am an Assistant Professor of Economics at Chungnam National University, where I teach econometrics, technology economics, and macroeconomics. My research develops spatial econometric methods and applies them to questions of firm innovation, digital transformation, regional development, and energy policy. I also served as Future Strategy Director for the RISE (Regional Innovation System & Ecosystem) project in the Chungcheong region. Previously, I was an Assistant Professor at Gachon University and a Research Fellow at the Korea Development Institute (KDI). I received my Ph.D. in Economics from Rice University, under the supervision of Professor Robin Sickles.

Strategic Technology Adoption and the Productivity Paradox
Economics of Innovation and New Technology (Forthcoming)
SSCI Q1
Why do technological revolutions fail to appear in productivity statistics? This paper develops a game-theoretic model in which firms with heterogeneous absorptive capacity make technology adoption decisions under competitive pressure. In equilibrium, high-capability firms adopt while low-capability firms either wait or adopt at a net loss due to competitive pressure. Aggregation across firm types generates a macro-level productivity paradox: substantial micro-level technological change produces negligible aggregate productivity gains. We provide micro-foundations for the Solow Paradox and derive three results: (i) a separating equilibrium in which only frontier firms adopt, generating the paradox as a composition effect; (ii) a pooling equilibrium in which competitive pressure forces unprofitable adoption; and (iii) multiple equilibria with coordination failure, where the Pareto-dominant equilibrium may not be selected. A welfare analysis shows that the social planner's optimal policy depends critically on whether non-adoption reflects coordination failure or genuine capability deficit. A formal decomposition shows that this composition channel is independent of, and additive to, the measurement channel identified in the directed technological change literature. We further show that workforce aging structurally expands the parameter space where the paradox persists, generating testable predictions for economies undergoing rapid demographic transitions.

디지털 전환 지향성의 강도와 다양성이 제조기업 성과에 미치는 이질적 효과
Heterogeneous Effects of the Intensity and Diversity of Digital Transformation Orientation on Manufacturing Firm Performance · 기술혁신학회지 (Forthcoming, June 2026)
KCI
This study examines how digital transformation (DX) orientation affects firm performance in Korean listed manufacturing firms, with attention to heterogeneous effects across profitability levels. We construct two text-based indices, DX Intensity and DX Diversity, via three-stage text mining on annual reports of 1,897 firms from DART (2016–2024). Applying the Correlated Random Effects (CRE) model to 1,507 firms and 8,135 firm-year observations (2019–2024), we separate between-firm selection from within-firm effects. The within-firm effect of DX Intensity is insignificant; the negative association in standard models reflects selection bias, as less profitable firms mention DX more actively. The key finding is that increasing DX Diversity significantly lowers ROA for low-profitability firms (−0.646), driven by advanced manufacturing (−0.792), suggesting that increasing DX diversity in resource-constrained firms is significantly associated with lower ROA. This effect is negligible for medium- and high-profitability firms, calling for differentiated DX policies based on firms' financial readiness.

전력시장 규제 구조와 재생에너지 전환: OECD PMR 전력 설문을 바탕으로
Electricity Market Regulation and Renewable Energy Transition: Evidence from the OECD PMR Electricity Questionnaire · Journal of Industrial Studies (산업연구) (Forthcoming, December 2026)
KCI
Renewable energy deployment for decarbonization varies widely across countries, and the regulatory structure of electricity markets may play a role. This study disaggregates individual items from the OECD PMR electricity questionnaire and applies Factor-Augmented Bayesian Model Averaging (FA-BMA) to a panel of 53 countries to identify which regulatory dimensions are associated with renewable energy transition. The results show that among four regulatory dimensions, only government ownership and control has significant explanatory power, and this relationship is not captured by the OECD's existing aggregate indicator. Korea has seen no change in this dimension for 15 years, and its wind and solar share remains the lowest among OECD countries, suggesting that regulatory reform, not just changes in policy instruments, is essential for accelerating the energy transition.

Standing on AlphaGo's Shoulders: Breakthrough Events and the Ladder Effect in AI Innovation
Technological Forecasting and Social Change
R&R SSCI Q1
When do breakthrough demonstrations catalyze sustained innovation? Conventional models treat technological progress as gradual accumulation, but pivotal events may reconfigure innovation landscapes by resolving uncertainty and coordinating decentralized actors. We exploit AlphaGo's March 2016 victory as a natural experiment, using synthetic control methods on 204,474 US AI patent filings and 35 control technologies. We find an immediate 8-fold surge (ATT = 1,436 patents/month, p = 0.032) that persisted for years. To explain how this effect operated, we develop the height relativity effect (HRE), decomposing the demonstration's impact into three stages. First, attention shifted heterogeneously: the surge concentrated in adjacent subfields such as computer vision and neural networks rather than in the originating domains of reinforcement learning and game AI, distinguishing the demonstration channel from supply-side tool shocks like TensorFlow. Second, new actors were mobilised: AI attracted 2.6 times more new patent assignees than before, while the median emerging technology experienced a decline. Third, these redirected efforts produced genuine capability realisation: technical vocabularies expanded dramatically, 472 novel term combinations emerged, and AI–biotechnology hybrids exhibited a 30.9% novelty rate. Grant date analysis confirms that these innovations survived USPTO examiner scrutiny. Our findings challenge innovation exhaustion perspectives and show that high-visibility demonstrations unlock collective effort not uniformly but through selective attention reallocation, entry barrier reduction, and cross-domain recombination. For innovation policy, demonstration projects should be designed with spillover potential to neighbouring domains in mind, complementing traditional R&D subsidies that address financial constraints but not feasibility uncertainty.

Green Patents, Human Capital, and Carbon Emissions in China: Evidence from a Spatial Durbin Model
with Zhimin Yan & Deokhyun Ryu · Under review at Energy Policy
Under Review SSCI Q1
This study examines how green patents reduce carbon emissions across Chinese provinces and why this effect varies by region. Using panel data for 30 provinces from 2006 to 2023, we estimate a Spatial Durbin Model that decomposes the direct and indirect effects of green innovation on carbon emissions. Two findings stand out. First, green patents consistently reduce emissions, and their indirect spillover effects across provincial borders exceed their direct local effects—suggesting that the carbon benefits of green innovation are primarily a regional, not a local, phenomenon. Second, the emission-reducing impact of green patents is significantly amplified in provinces with higher human capital, indicating that human capital appears to be an important moderator of how effectively green technology translates into actual emission reductions. These findings call for a reorientation of climate policy: investing in the human capacity to absorb and deploy green technologies is at least as important as expanding R&D budgets.
